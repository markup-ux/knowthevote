State · Vote for one

Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials. An official legislative description of the office says the governor is vested with the supreme executive power of the state and has direct financial supervision over all state departments.

Zach Lahn

Republican

Farmer, businessman, and sixth-generation Iowan running for governor.

He says he is a father and his own largest donor, and that he answers to his family and to Iowans rather than to corporations. He says he wants Iowa to be a place where his children and grandchildren can stay and build a life.

  • He says he will free Iowa's farmers from a system that leaves the farmer last, and break up monopolies that treat farmers and families as numbers.
  • He says more than 25 percent of Iowa land is owned by out-of-state investors and foreign governments, and he will fight to keep that land in Iowa hands.
  • He says a classroom should be for learning rather than political doctrine, and that anyone who uses a teaching job to push political doctrine will not teach in the state.

zachlahn.com · Donate

Rob Sand

Democratic

Iowa state auditor running for governor.

He says Iowa now has the worst economy in the nation, public schools that have slipped to the middle of the pack, and the country's fastest growth in cancer. He says he has proposed mandatory prison for theft of taxpayer money every year since he became state auditor, and that a 2023 law let agencies hide documents from that office.

  • He says he would ban stock trading by the governor, legislators, and other covered officials, require personal financial disclosures and blind trusts, and make violations carry mandatory prison time.
  • He says anyone who steals more than $10,000 in taxpayer dollars should face mandatory prison, a ban on doing business with the state, and loss of taxpayer-funded retirement and health benefits.
  • He says he would restore powers taken from the auditor in 2023, extend whistleblower protections beyond state employees, and require annual audits, tuition limits, and income limits for the private-school voucher program.

Federal · Vote for one

Senate

The winner is one of 100 votes on federal law, spending, treaties, and judges. The term is six years. Senate committees gather information through investigations and hearings in order to recommend legislation, and they evaluate presidential nominees for executive and judicial posts.

Ashley Hinson

Republican

U.S. representative from northeast Iowa running for the Senate.

She is a mother and a former journalist who previously represented Iowa's 67th House district, including Hiawatha, Robins, Cedar Rapids, and Marion, where she says she lives. In Congress she serves on Appropriations, the Select Committee on Communist China, and the Ethics Committee.

  • She says she is working with President Trump to secure the border, cut wasteful spending, and pursue pro-growth policies.
  • She says she has introduced bipartisan bills to improve child care for working families and to ban federal lobbying as a check on corruption.
  • She says that on Appropriations she works to account for every taxpayer dollar, and that on the China committee she works to hold the Chinese Communist Party accountable.

ashleyhinson.com · Donate

Josh Turek

Democratic

Iowa state legislator and two-time Paralympic gold medalist running for the Senate.

Born with spina bifida after his father was exposed to Agent Orange in Vietnam, he says he had 21 surgeries by age 12 and later played for the United States at four Paralympics, including a gold medal in Tokyo in 2021. He then won a Council Bluffs seat in the Iowa House, a district he says Donald Trump had carried, by six votes.

  • He says economic policy should benefit the middle class and workers rather than Wall Street and billionaire donors.
  • He says he supports public education and opposes school vouchers.
  • He says Iowa's health care system needs major reform, and he supports protection of air, food, and water.

Thomas Laehn

Libertarian

Greene County attorney and the first Libertarian elected to a partisan office in Iowa, running for the Senate.

He grew up in Allison, taught political science and constitutional law after study at Drake, Louisiana State, and Hebrew University, then earned a law degree at the University of Iowa. He was elected Greene County attorney in 2018 and reelected as a Libertarian in 2022.

  • He says the two major parties have taken political power from the people and that he would work to give that power back.
  • As county attorney he says he prosecuted child rape, attempted murder, and other violent crime, and also built a community-service program for nonviolent offenders.
  • He says that program, and his leadership, drove recidivism down.

State · Vote for one

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law. Iowa law directs the attorney general to prosecute and defend causes in the appellate courts in which the state is a party or interested, and to supervise county attorneys.

Brenna Bird

Republican

Iowa attorney general and former Guthrie County attorney.

The campaign says Iowans elected her attorney general and that she is the first Republican to hold the office since 1979. She was Guthrie County attorney from 2018, a prosecutor since 2016, and president of the Iowa County Attorneys Association, and she previously was counsel to Governor Terry Branstad.

  • She says she has worked to get criminals off the street, advocate for crime victims, and stand with law enforcement.
  • She says that as counsel to Governor Branstad she fought federal health-care policy and rules from other states that she says tried to dictate Iowa farm practices.
  • She says she led a fight against EPA regulations that were hurting Iowa farmers and will use the office to defend what she calls the rule of law.

birdforiowa.com · Donate

Nate Willems

Democratic

Labor lawyer running for Iowa attorney general.

He is a lifelong Iowan and a father who says he has spent his career taking on corporations over stolen wages, outsourced jobs, and workplace safety. He says that work has covered tens of thousands of Iowans.

  • He says he will go after wage theft, worker misclassification, and unpaid overtime.
  • He says he will hold corporations accountable for cheating families, work with prosecutors on violent crime, and protect seniors from scams and nursing-home safety failures.
  • He says the office is the one that can fine corporate polluters who contaminate air and water, and that it should focus on Iowa rather than national political fights.

State · Vote for one

Secretary of State

The secretary of state runs the duties this state assigns that office. Those duties are not the same in every state. The secretary of state keeps the acts and resolutions of the General Assembly and the enrolled state constitutions, and the code designates that officer as the state commissioner of elections.

Paul Pate

Republican

Iowa secretary of state serving a third term.

He is a small-business owner from Linn County and a former state senator and mayor of Cedar Rapids. Colleagues elected him president of the National Association of Secretaries of State in 2019.

  • He says he made it easier for service members overseas to vote, started online voter registration, and brought voter ID to Iowa.
  • He says he started the Safe at Home program for survivors of violence and has pushed election cybersecurity and ballot access for Iowans with disabilities.
  • He says business-filing changes have cut bureaucracy and sped processing for Iowa companies, under a slogan of easy to vote and hard to cheat.

pateforiowa.com · Donate

Ryan Peterman

Democratic

Former naval officer running for secretary of state.

He says he served more than a decade as a naval officer, led sailors overseas, worked on defense policy in the Senate, and helped develop officers at the Naval Academy. He says he is back in Iowa and running to do the elections job without being a partisan warrior.

  • He says the job is to make sure every eligible Iowan can vote and that elections are fair and secure.
  • He says he has met county auditors across the state and wants to be a partner they can trust.
  • He says trust in the office depends on keeping politics out of the work.

District 1

Mariannette Miller-Meeks

Republican

Doctor, Army veteran, and U.S. representative running for re-election.

She says she is the fourth of eight children, left home at 16 for community college, and enlisted in the Army at 18, rising from private to lieutenant colonel over 24 years. She later practiced medicine in Ottumwa, served in the Iowa Senate, and was elected to Congress in 2020.

  • She says she is running for re-election because Iowans deserve someone who will keep fighting for them and for Iowa values.
  • On her 2026 tour she says she is fighting for lower health-care costs, lower taxes, and common sense.

Christina Bohannan

Democratic

Law professor, engineer, and former state representative running for Congress.

She says she is a mom running so Iowa's working families come ahead of the wealthy and well-connected. After 26 years teaching constitutional law at the University of Iowa, she says she feels a duty to defend self-government against money and partisanship.

  • She says her top priority is lowering the cost of living: end tariffs she says raise family costs, stop hedge funds from buying family homes, build more housing, let Medicare negotiate more drug prices, and cap insulin at $35 a month.
  • She says she takes no corporate PAC money and would ban stock trading by members of Congress and other high officials, seek a constitutional amendment to overturn Citizens United, and set term limits.
  • She says her father's insurer canceled coverage after he became ill, and she wants a health system that does not drop people when they get sick.

Joe Mitchell

Republican

Former Iowa legislator and housing developer running for Congress.

Born and raised south of Cedar Rapids, he says he was sworn into the Iowa House at 21, the youngest state legislator in state history, and later worked on housing at HUD and the Federal Housing Finance Agency. He develops workforce housing in rural Iowa and says younger Iowans should be able to afford to stay.

  • He says he takes no corporate PAC money and supports term limits of 12 years in the House and 12 in the Senate, plus a ban on members of Congress trading stocks.
  • He says immigration policy should put Americans first, including a secure border, removal of people he describes as criminal illegal immigrants, and a temporary ban on H-1B visas.
  • He says deregulating homebuilding would lower home prices, and he wants lower taxes for middle-class families and small businesses and fewer rules on farmers.

Democratic

Chaplain and Iowa state representative running for Congress.

She says public service began as a chaplain beside people in their hardest hours, and that as a state representative she has argued government should serve people rather than the powerful. She is running to level the playing field for Iowans who are choosing between rent, food, medicine, and care for family.

  • She says no one should go bankrupt or ration medicine because of a medical emergency, and that rural health care needs urgent changes as cancer rates rise.
  • She says money in politics has fueled partisanship, and that getting money out of politics is necessary for a healthier system.
  • She says Iowa farmers cannot wait another decade for a farm bill, and she opposes private-school vouchers she says benefit the most fortunate.

Combat veteran and U.S. representative for Iowa's Third District.

A sixth-generation Iowan, husband, and father of six, he says he flew more than 700 combat hours after 9/11, served on the National Security Council, and later served in the Iowa House and Senate before winning this U.S. House seat in 2022.
