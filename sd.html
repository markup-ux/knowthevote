State · Vote for one

Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials. The South Dakota Constitution makes the governor responsible for the faithful execution of the law, and the secretary of state's candidate list titles this contest Governor and Lieutenant Governor and lists each party's nominees as a pair.

Larry Rhoden

Republican

Governor of South Dakota

Larry Rhoden's campaign says he is a fourth-generation South Dakotan and a rancher who served 16 years in the legislature and six years as lieutenant governor. It says he became governor and is running to stay in the office.

  • He says his mission is to keep South Dakota strong, safe, and free, with less government and less red tape.
  • He says his legislative record focused on low taxes, strong property rights, and personal freedom.
  • He says he would stand up for law enforcement and for property rights, and keep the state open for business.

Democratic

Business owner and former state legislator running for governor

Dan Ahlers's campaign says he is a business owner, a former Dell Rapids Chamber of Commerce administrator, and a former state legislator who served on the Appropriations Committee. He says he would use that experience as governor.

Senate

The winner is one of 100 votes on federal law, spending, treaties, and judges. The term is six years.

Mike Rounds

Republican

U.S. senator and former governor

Mike Rounds's campaign says he is a former business owner, state senator, and two-term governor who now serves in the U.S. Senate. He says he is running for reelection.

  • He says he stands with an America First agenda and with President Trump, and that he will back policies that put South Dakota and the country first.
  • He says that as a member of the Armed Services Committee he works to keep the military the best in the world.
  • He says he supports local control of education, wants to eliminate the U.S. Department of Education, and supports the Second Amendment.

roundsforsenate.com · Donate

Brian Bengs

Independent

Independent candidate for U.S. Senate

Brian Bengs is running as an independent for the U.S. Senate. His site says he was a National Park Ranger and that he has signed a pledge against money from artificial-intelligence data centers.

  • He says he wants a constitutional amendment to ban unlimited corporate political spending that he traces to the Citizens United decision.
  • He says local communities should be able to refuse a new data center, and that he will not take data-center money.
  • He says he supports term limits for Congress and that he would not vote to continue what he calls an unnecessary war with Iran.

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law. South Dakota law assigns the attorney general to appear for the state in the Supreme Court and to advise and supervise the state's attorneys.

Lance Russell

Republican

State's attorney for Fall River and Oglala Lakota counties

Lance Russell's campaign says he is the longtime state's attorney for Fall River County and Oglala Lakota County and that he won the Republican nomination at the state party convention. The site describes him as a proponent of law and order.

  • He says tough-on-crime policies work.
  • He says his daily work is meant to protect the people of South Dakota.

Secretary of State

The secretary of state runs the duties this state assigns that office. Those duties are not the same in every state. South Dakota law charges the secretary of state with the great seal, filing official acts, and publishing the election laws.

Heather Baxter

Republican

State representative and small-business owner

Heather Baxter's campaign says she has been the state representative for District 34 since November 2024 and owns a small business in Rapid City and Custer. She says she is a lifelong South Dakotan running for secretary of state.

  • She says she would push secure and transparent elections and restore trust in how votes are run.
  • She says she would give small businesses easier access to office resources and cut red tape through what she calls SD DOGE.
  • She says she would improve customer service in the secretary of state's office.

Terrence Davis

Democratic

Former state trooper running for secretary of state

Terrence Davis's campaign says he is a former state trooper who worked out of Sioux Falls and is the Democratic candidate for secretary of state. The site says the current Republican secretary of state, Monae Johnson, endorsed him.

  • He says he trusts South Dakota's election system and will protect it.
  • His stated standard is that every vote is counted and every voice is heard.

davisforsouthdakota.com · Donate

Tamara Lesnar

Libertarian

Libertarian candidate for secretary of state

Tamara Lesnar's campaign says the Libertarian Party nominated her for secretary of state and that earlier campaigns of hers focused on civil liberties and civil rights. She says the office should keep elections, administration, and business services running smoothly.

  • She says she would make voting places easier to reach, curb what she calls double taxation, and build a statewide database of ballot-initiative signatures.
  • She says she would file lawsuits against laws she believes violate the Bill of Rights.
  • She criticizes sharing voter lists with federal agencies and giving the Postal Service a role in deciding who receives an absentee ballot.
