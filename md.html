Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials.

Dan Cox

Republican

Candidate for governor of Maryland.

Dan Cox's campaign describes him as a husband, father, and small-business owner who says he has taken on leaders in both parties. He says he is running because high prices and the cost of living are weighing on Maryland families.

  • He says he would cut taxes and fees and limit property taxes so retirees are not taxed out of their homes.
  • He proposes down-payment help and tax credits for first-time and younger buyers, and says building and permitting costs should come down.
  • He says Maryland should reopen closed power plants, study small modular reactors, and give households more energy choices so electric bills stop climbing.

Wes Moore

Democratic

Governor of Maryland, running for reelection.

Wes Moore's campaign says he is Maryland's 63rd governor, a combat veteran who led soldiers in Afghanistan, and a former chief executive of the Robin Hood Foundation. He says his work in office has centered on child poverty, the middle class, and public education.

  • He says he has cut taxes for veterans and middle-class families and wants more Marylanders to be able to afford to live in the state.
  • He points to record spending on public schools and says he wants those schools to be the best in the country.
  • He says public safety is a priority and that homicides in Baltimore are at their lowest level in 50 years.

Andy Ellis

Other

Green Party candidate for governor of Maryland.

Andy Ellis says he has lived in Baltimore since 2005, coached debate, and spent nearly 15 years in corporate technology. He says he has spent more than a decade organizing the Green Party in Baltimore and across Maryland.

  • He says the state should stop data-center projects that he argues threaten communities, water, and public health.
  • He says unaffiliated and third-party voters are shut out of meaningful elections and that he has a plan aimed at two-party political corruption.
  • He says the state should spread wealth, power, and opportunity so more residents have a say in incentives, public funds, and budgets.

Cathy White

Other

Working Class Party candidate for governor of Maryland.

Cathy White says she is not a professional politician and has spent 40 years as a factory production worker. She says she cared for her mother and for a sister with Down syndrome while working full time.

  • She says workers need their own political organization because neither major party represents them on jobs, low wages, and rising prices.
  • She says child care and elder care cost more than most workers can pay, and that many older workers cannot afford to retire.
  • She says workers have no reason to support the current war in the Middle East, which she argues enriches defense and energy companies while raising the price of gas.

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law.

James Rutledge III

Republican

Candidate for Maryland attorney general.

Jim Rutledge's campaign says he earned a law degree with honors from the University of Maryland and has practiced law in Maryland for decades. It says he served as a Bel Air town commissioner and on the judicial nominating commission for Harford County's district and circuit courts.

  • He says he would fight crime at every level without turning the office into a partisan fight.
  • He says the office should go after hackers, online predators, and scams that target older and vulnerable people.
  • He says he would protect clean water and the Chesapeake Bay.

Campaign site · Donate

Anthony Brown

Democratic

Maryland attorney general, running for reelection.

Anthony Brown's campaign says he is the state's attorney general, an Army veteran, and a lawyer raised by immigrant parents. It says that before this office he served in the legislature, as lieutenant governor, and in Congress.

  • He says he created a civil-rights division and widened the Independent Investigations Division so it can prosecute police-involved deaths.
  • He says he has defended gun-safety laws in court and pursued wage theft and corporate conduct that harms workers and consumers.
  • He says he has sued when federal policy threatened environmental rules, voting rights, and protections for women and immigrants.

District 1

Andy Harris

Republican

Physician and U.S. representative who chairs the House Freedom Caucus.

The son of immigrants who fled communist Eastern Europe after World War II, he says he has been a Johns Hopkins physician, a Naval Reserve veteran, a state senator, and, since 2025, an eighth-term congressman. He chairs the Agriculture appropriations subcommittee.

  • He says he is fighting to restore fiscal responsibility in Washington and protect taxpayer dollars.
  • The campaign describes him as a common-sense conservative with the courage to change Washington.
  • He says he is unafraid to speak for the priorities of Maryland's 1st District.

Dan Schwartz

Democratic

Consumer advocate running for Congress in Maryland's 1st District.

His campaign says he is a Democrat who has spent more than 14 years working with state banking regulators and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau to challenge financial fraud. He and his wife Megan live in Bellevue, and he says her work as a nurse practitioner in Dorchester County shapes his view of health-care costs.

  • He says he would hold monthly town halls and support a ban on members of Congress trading stocks, options, and crypto.
  • He says he would fight spending freezes and tariffs that hurt farmers and raise prices, and protect consumers from what he calls corporate grift.
  • He says he would restore safety programs for food, water, bridges, and air travel, protect the environment, and defend affordable care and public-health research.

District 2

Dave Wallace

Republican

Small-business owner running for Congress in Maryland's 2nd District.

His campaign says he is a lifelong Marylander with a biology degree who has spent more than 40 years building a family business, starting in 1981. It says he is not a career politician and has also worked through a group he calls Restore America's Mission.

  • He says he will vote to equip police and other first responders, and that he would sponsor a gold-standard bill to stop the dollar from losing value.
  • His mission statement calls for restoring constitutional freedoms, a free-market economy, public safety, and national sovereignty.
  • He says that work includes opposing socialism and what he describes as strict Sharia law and movements that weaken faith, family, and the Bill of Rights.

Johnny Olszewski Jr.

Democratic

U.S. representative for Maryland's 2nd District since 2025.

A lifelong Marylander, he says working-class values from eastern Baltimore County guide him. He serves on Small Business and Foreign Affairs and on the Democratic Steering and Policy Committee.

  • He says he wants higher wages, affordable health care, and fully funded public schools, and he opposed a budget he says gave tax breaks to the wealthy while threatening programs families use.
  • He says he would extend health-insurance tax credits, end tariffs that raise prices, cut drug costs, expand housing, and protect Medicare, Medicaid, SNAP, and the Child Tax Credit.
  • He says he supports expanding voting rights, protecting court independence, and banning stock trading by members of Congress.

Democratic

U.S. representative and the youngest woman ever elected to Congress from Maryland.

She says she learned her work ethic in a union household, served six years in the Maryland Senate, and lives in Annapolis with her husband Eric. She says her first term included five bills signed into law and more than 2,000 constituent cases.

  • She says she is working to revoke tariffs that raise prices, expand affordable child care, ease student loans, build more housing, and roll back utility rate increases.
  • She says affordability is personal as a first-time homeowner and young professional with student loans.
  • She says she is also standing up for federal workers, supporting veterans, and protecting the Chesapeake Bay.

Republican

Builder and farmer running for Congress in Maryland's 5th District.

His campaign says he is a husband, father, and grandfather, the son of a veteran, who started as a union electrician, studied at the University of Maryland, and has run a home-building business since 1991. He says he is also a farmer and a conservative running in Southern Maryland.

Republican

Conservative candidate running for Congress in Maryland's 8th District.

Her campaign says she is a Christian conservative who grew up in Montgomery County and is running on an America First platform. She says she has watched schools, taxes, and crime move in the wrong direction.
