Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials. The Connecticut Constitution makes the governor captain general of the state militia, except when the militia is called into United States service.

Ryan Fazio

Republican

Connecticut state senator

He says he was born in Norwalk, raised in Greenwich, and earned an economics degree from Northwestern University before working in energy, agriculture, and shipping. He also served on Greenwich's Representative Town Meeting and its Energy Efficiency Committee.

  • He says he co-authored a 2023 bill to disclose hidden utility taxes.
  • He says he co-authored a 2025 bill to reduce public-benefits charges on electric bills.
  • He says he authored a 2024 bill supporting municipal home rule.

Ned Lamont

Democratic

Governor of Connecticut

He is the governor and a former small-business owner who says he built a Connecticut company and learned what it takes to meet a payroll. He says that in office he has worked on the state's finances, a middle-class tax cut, public education, and affordable childcare.

  • He says he is running again to keep lowering costs and expanding opportunity.

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law. Connecticut law gives the attorney general general supervision over legal matters in which the state is an interested party, except matters directed by prosecuting officers.

John Bolton

Republican

Connecticut attorney and former Westport planning and zoning commissioner

He says he is a Connecticut attorney and a former member of the Westport Planning and Zoning Commission. He says he grew up as the son of a longtime auditor in the Connecticut State Auditors of Public Accounts.

  • He says he would make utility rate cases a priority and challenge policies that raise electric costs.
  • He says he would investigate AI-driven pricing and pursue consumer fraud, including scams aimed at seniors.
  • He says he would hold neighboring states accountable when pollution harms Connecticut rivers, drinking water, or Long Island Sound.

William Tong

Democratic

Attorney General of Connecticut

He says he was born and raised in the Hartford area, practiced as a litigator for 18 years, and served 12 years in the Connecticut House, including as chair of the Judiciary and Banking committees. He says he was elected attorney general in 2018.

Secretary of State

The secretary of state runs the duties this state assigns that office. Those duties are not the same in every state. Connecticut law makes the secretary the keeper of the public records and of the state seal.

Peter Lumaj

Republican

Business owner running for secretary of the state

He describes decades of business experience and says he has created jobs while trying to reduce bureaucracy. His campaign says he would put families first.

  • He says he would reduce taxes and cut red tape to grow the state's economy.
  • He says he wants conditions where families can thrive.
  • He says he would defend constitutional rights and conservative principles.

Stephanie Thomas

Democratic

Connecticut secretary of the state

She was sworn in as secretary of the state on January 4, 2023, after advising nonprofits, running a business, and serving as a state representative for Norwalk, Wilton, and Westport. She says she was the first Black person elected secretary of the state of Connecticut.

  • She says she led the rollout of early voting and secured funding to replace aging voting tabulators.
  • She says the office is building a centralized voter registration system and carrying out the John R. Lewis Voting Rights Act.
  • She says she started a monthly newsletter of funding and training resources for business owners.
