Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials.

Andy Biggs

Republican

U.S. representative

Andy Biggs is an Arizona native who has represented the state's 5th Congressional District since 2016. He is running for governor with Sine Kerr for lieutenant governor.

  • He says he will eliminate the state income tax, work with local governments to ease property taxes, and cut permitting barriers for new homes.
  • He says he will end state incentives for data centers and set standards to protect Arizona's water and energy.
  • He says he will reconvene a Border Strike Force aimed at human trafficking and drug trafficking and equip police to fight drug trafficking.

Katie Hobbs

Democratic

Governor of Arizona

Katie Hobbs is a social worker who was elected Arizona secretary of state and then governor, taking office in 2022. Her campaign says that before politics she worked with homeless youth and helped run a large domestic-violence shelter.

  • She says she eliminated the tax on rent, offered down-payment help for first homes, and cut red tape to make housing more affordable.
  • She says she created a Border Coordination Office, sent more than $100 million in border-security funding to local police, and that Task Force SAFE has stopped over 19 million fentanyl pills.
  • She says a water compromise passed under her watch will create jobs, lower housing costs, and conserve water, and that she ended a foreign corporation's groundwater leases.

katiehobbs.org · Donate

Risa Lombardo

Other

Candidate for governor

Risa Lombardo says she is running for governor to speak for people she believes the major parties ignore. She says she has been registered with both major parties and spent most of her life as a Democrat.

  • Her issues page lists the Green Party call to cut the military budget sharply and to move from fossil fuels to renewable energy.
  • That page also demands a living wage and a stronger safety net.
  • It calls for public financing of elections, open debates, and voting systems she describes as more representative.

risalombardo.com

Teri Ann Hourihan

Other

No Labels candidate for governor of Arizona.

Her campaign says she was born and raised in Glendale, later earned a doctorate, and became a licensed counselor who founded a behavioral-health company. She says she is running after a Medicaid payment suspension she describes as unjust, and that she is not a career politician.

  • She says she takes no PAC or special-interest money and wants state government to be more accessible and accountable to residents.
  • She says she and her running mate are focused on lowering the cost of living and acting for Arizona families rather than either major party.
  • On schools, she says she would keep school choice, including empowerment scholarship accounts, and audit education spending so more money reaches classrooms.

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law.

Warren Petersen

Republican

President of the Arizona Senate

Warren Petersen is president of the Arizona Senate and the Republican candidate for attorney general. His campaign says that in the Senate he defended state laws, opposed mandates he calls unconstitutional, and backed Second Amendment rights.

  • He says as attorney general he will uphold the rule of law and stand with law enforcement to hold criminals accountable.
  • He says he will protect the rights of Arizona families.
  • He says he has worked to keep men out of women's sports and to check government power.

petersenforag.com · Donate

Kris Mayes

Democratic

Arizona attorney general

Kris Mayes is Arizona's attorney general. Her campaign calls her a consumer advocate and says she is the second woman, and the first mother, to serve as the state's chief law-enforcement officer and chief attorney.

  • Her campaign says she took on Saudi-owned mega-farms over Arizona's water and corporations she says gouged people on rent and groceries.
  • She says she has gone after drug smugglers, elder abusers, and scammers, and that her priorities include protecting rural groundwater, prosecuting sexual predators, and cracking down on AI that targets kids.
  • Her priorities page also lists overturning tariffs, protecting $2.3 billion in Arizona tax dollars, and protecting Arizonans' freedoms.

krismayes.com · Donate

State · Vote for one

Secretary of State

The secretary of state runs the duties this state assigns that office. Those duties are not the same in every state.

Alexander Kolodin

Republican

Candidate for secretary of state

Alexander Kolodin is running for Arizona secretary of state. His campaign says accountability in that office starts with securing elections and reporting results voters can trust.

  • He says he will harden election systems against manipulation, error, and interference so every legal vote is counted accurately.
  • He says he will run elections that reflect voters' choices without bureaucratic overreach or partisan interference.
  • He says he will modernize administration so results come faster without giving up accuracy, and he promises more transparency.

alexforaz.com · Donate

Adrian Fontes

Democratic

Arizona secretary of state

Adrian Fontes, a former Marine who served on active duty from 1992 to 1996, was elected Maricopa County recorder in 2016 and Arizona's 21st secretary of state in 2022. He previously prosecuted cases in Denver, at the Maricopa County Attorney's Office, and in the Arizona attorney general's international prosecution unit.

  • He says he has enforced Arizona's voter ID laws, kept voter rolls current, and will keep defending free and fair elections.
  • He says he is a defender of vote-by-mail, which his campaign says more than 80 percent of Arizonans use, and that he has gone to court to block demands for voters' private data.
  • He says he is cutting red tape and wait times in the office's business services.

electfontes.com · Donate

Other

His Clean Elections statement says he grew up in a small farming town in California, worked in construction, labor, sales, and service, and settled in Arizona with his wife and two children.

Jay Feely

Republican

Former NFL kicker running for Congress in Arizona's 1st District.

His campaign says he played 14 seasons in the NFL, including for the Arizona Cardinals, and later worked as a CBS analyst. He and his wife Rebecca have lived in the Valley for 15 years and are raising four children.

  • He says he will protect Arizona's water, stand with farmers and ranchers, defend Second Amendment rights, and oppose federal mandates he says hurt families, small businesses, and schools.

  • He says he will cut red tape, extend the Trump-era tax cuts, and back domestic manufacturing so the Valley keeps higher-paying jobs.

Democratic

Emergency physician and former state representative running for Congress in Arizona's 1st District.

His campaign says he is an emergency doctor who won three terms in the Arizona House and had more bills signed than any Democrat in the legislature over the last decade. He says that work focused on teacher pay, small businesses, and public health.

  • He says he would roll back tariffs he calls a tax on groceries and gas, and protect Social Security, Medicare, and Medicaid.
