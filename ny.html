Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials. New York's certified ballot pairs the governor and lieutenant governor on one ticket, and the voter chooses one pair.

Bruce Blakeman

Republican

Nassau County executive running for governor.

His campaign describes him as a lifelong New Yorker who has also served as a Port Authority commissioner. It says that as county executive he hired more police officers and did not raise county property or sales taxes.

  • He says public safety is the center of the campaign, including opposition to sanctuary policies and work with federal immigration officers to remove people he calls criminals.
  • He says he blocked scheduled property-tax increases in Nassau County and would seek relief for homeowners and renters as governor.
  • His site names Madison County Sheriff Todd Hood as his lieutenant governor running mate and describes Hood as a career law-enforcement officer.

Kathy Hochul

Democratic

Governor of New York, seeking another term.

Her campaign calls her New York's first mom governor and says she has focused on putting money back in people's pockets, lowering costs, and keeping New Yorkers safe. She is running with New York City Council Speaker Adrienne Adams.

  • She points to inflation refund checks, a larger child tax credit, middle-class tax cuts, and work toward universal child care and lower auto insurance costs.
  • She highlights free school meals, a ban on cell phones during the school day, and gun laws she says are meant to make streets and subways safer.
  • She says the ticket would keep lowering costs, invest in community safety, and protect New York from what she calls MAGA extremism.

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law. The office describes New York's attorney general as the state's chief lawyer and law enforcement officer.

Saritha Komatireddy

Republican

Former federal prosecutor running for attorney general.

Her campaign says that after the September 11 attacks she went to law school and spent more than a decade prosecuting terrorism, cybercrime, drug trafficking, and corruption cases. It says she later served as chief of staff at the Drug Enforcement Administration and is a mother of four.

  • She says the attorney general should enforce the law by prosecuting crime, and that safety is the office's first job.
  • She says she would get people who are homeless or sick off the street in a humane way.
  • She says she would go after corruption and fraud, and that she would work with police and district attorneys on repeat offenders, hate crimes, and fentanyl cases.

sarithafornewyork.com
