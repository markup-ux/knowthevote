Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials.

Mike Mazzei

Republican

Former state senator running for governor.

His campaign describes him as a business leader who focused on budgets, fiscal discipline, and where taxpayer money goes. He says faith, family, and freedom guide his decisions.

  • He says he would phase out the property tax for seniors and veterans while keeping essential services and the budget stable.
  • The site also lists protecting Oklahoma land, schools that teach students to read, and more high-paying jobs.
  • He says government should be accountable, respect taxpayers, and make daily life less expensive.

mikeforok.com

Cyndi Munson

Democratic

Candidate for governor of Oklahoma.

Her campaign says a campaign should be about people, and she is asking Oklahomans to send stories, photos, and letters about why they love the state. She says those stories would guide her if she becomes governor.

Lt. Governor

The lieutenant governor is next in line if the governor cannot finish the term. Any other duties are set by state law.

T. Shannon

Republican

Candidate for lieutenant governor and a former Speaker of the Oklahoma House.

His campaign describes him as a fourth-generation Oklahoman who has been a transportation commissioner, a bank chief executive, and a senior advisor in the Trump administration. He says he is running to bring that record to the lieutenant governor's office.

  • He says Oklahoma can lead in economic opportunity, energy, agriculture, education, and innovation while protecting the state's values.
  • The site lists growing the economy, cutting taxes and red tape, and supporting farmers and ranchers.
  • It also says he would back law enforcement and defend freedoms.

votetwshannon.com

Kelly Forbes

Democratic

Educator and consultant running for lieutenant governor.

His campaign says he is a lifelong Oklahoman raised in Skiatook and Broken Arrow, the first in his family to graduate from college, and the holder of a doctorate in administration and leadership. It says he has taught and administered schools, owns Kelly B's Consulting, and serves on the board of Oklahoma City's family justice center.

  • He says he would invest in small business, CareerTech, workforce training, and partnerships with Native nations, and would work on affordable housing and food insecurity.
  • He says he would put more funding into public schools, respect teachers, and keep public dollars in public schools.
  • He says he would expand health-care access in underserved places, promote tourism, and lead the office with public integrity rather than as a career politician.

forbesforoklahoma.com

Senate

The winner is one of 100 votes on federal law, spending, treaties, and judges. The term is six years.

Kevin Hern

Republican

Candidate for U.S. Senate who says he was born on an Air Force base.

His campaign says he earned an architectural drafting certificate in high school, used it to pay for an engineering degree, and later earned an MBA. It says that in office he has backed the Trump administration's agenda.

  • He says he has backed bills to recognize groups of veterans and a constitutional amendment aimed at a balanced budget.
  • He says he has voted to fund a border wall and to cut off federal money for sanctuary campuses, and that he wants small businesses to get capital.
  • He says he would protect religious liberty, restrict abortion funding, and protect gun rights, including concealed-carry reciprocity.

hernforsenate.com

N'Kiyla Jasmine Thomas

Democratic

Candidate for U.S. Senate who says the campaign takes no corporate PAC money.

Her site says she secured the Democratic nomination and is on the November 3 ballot. She says she is a military spouse and a Chickasaw citizen, and that her son is autistic.

  • She says the campaign is funded by individual Oklahomans and will not take corporate PAC money or money from AIPAC.
  • She lists honest education reform, health-care access for rural and underserved communities, and support for military families.
  • She also lists protections for Native sovereignty, equity in autism care, and transparency in government.

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law.

Jon Echols

Republican

Candidate for attorney general who says he is a fifth-generation Oklahoman and a small-business owner.

His campaign says his family still owns a farm in Okeene, that he and his wife are raising three children in Oklahoma City, and that he earned degrees from the University of Oklahoma and Oklahoma City University's law school. He says he started his first business in college.

  • He says everyone, including political elites, should be treated equally under the law, and that the office should back police and defend liberties.
  • He says he would watch taxpayer dollars and support border and fentanyl enforcement.
  • He says he would defend private property against foreign adversaries and out-of-state interests, work with tribal nations, and back the state's energy industry.

jonechols.com

Nick Coffey

Democratic

Former federal prosecutor running for attorney general.

His campaign calls him a lifelong Oklahoman and a career prosecutor, not a politician. It says that as a federal prosecutor in the Western District of Oklahoma he led complex cases and prosecuted transnational criminal organizations.

  • He says those cases covered money laundering, immigration, illegal firearms, and drug conspiracies.
  • He also lists counterfeiting and fraud among the crimes he has prosecuted, and says the work was about protecting communities and holding criminal organizations accountable.

coffeyforok.com

U.S. House.

Every district NBC News is publishing for this state. This list is not filtered to one address. A written note is added only from that candidate's own site.

District 1

Mark Tedford

Republican

Businessman and former state representative running for Congress.

His campaign says he is a Christian husband, father, and grandfather who spent more than 30 years building insurance and construction businesses in Tulsa. It says that in the state House he helped pass a large tax cut, immigration enforcement, and a homeowner bill of rights.

  • He says he would lower costs for working families by cutting inflationary spending, red tape, and energy and regulatory costs, and by restoring fiscal discipline.
  • He says every nation has a right to secure its borders, enforce its laws, and put its citizens first.
  • He says he is running to help build an economy that works for the people who fund it and to bring good jobs back.

tedfordforcongress.com

John Croisant

Democratic

Former teacher, coach, and local businessman running for Congress.

His campaign says he taught at Edison Prep, coached, ran a local business, and is a parent who wants government to be accountable to families. He says he is running to be heard as a voice for hardworking Oklahomans.

  • He says he would lower costs so families are not choosing between bills and care, and that hard work should be enough to own a home, send children to college, and retire with dignity.
  • On schools, he says public money should stay in public schools with accountability, and he supports a program to help people earn education degrees and teach for five years debt-free.
  • He says he would keep illegal guns off the streets, expand affordable care so people are not skipping treatment, and back term limits, an end to congressional stock trading, and tighter lobbying rules.

croisantforcongress.com

District 2

Josh Brecheen

Republican

U.S. representative and fourth-generation rancher from eastern Oklahoma.

His official biography says he owned a small excavation and trucking business, served in the Oklahoma Senate from 2010 to 2018, and earlier worked for U.S. Senator Tom Coburn. He is a graduate of Oklahoma State University and was elected to Congress in 2022.

  • He says he is focused on reining in unsustainable debt and deficit spending, which he believes is undermining national security.
  • His House site lists his legislative priorities as deficit spending, border security, traditional values, constitutional adherence, and energy dominance.

brecheen.house.gov

Brandon Wade

Democratic

Union leader and Democrat running for Congress.

His campaign says he was raised in Copan, returned to Bartlesville as a third-generation union worker, and spent 26 years in leadership with IUOE Local 351. He says he is the son of a Vietnam Army veteran and serves as secretary of the Oklahoma Democratic Party Veterans Federation.

  • He says health care should be affordable for everyone and not tied to a job, and that he would protect women's health care choices and strengthen Social Security and Medicare.
  • He says the economy should grow from the working class up, with fair wages, safe workplaces, and corporations and the wealthy paying their fair share.
  • He says he would fully fund public schools, modernize immigration, and defend the rights of women, LGBTQ+ people, immigrants, and minorities.

brandon4congress.com

District 3

Frank Lucas

Republican

U.S. representative and fifth-generation Oklahoma farmer.

His official biography says his family has farmed in Oklahoma for more than 120 years, that he graduated from Oklahoma State University in agricultural economics, and that he was first elected to the House in a 1994 special election. Before Congress he served five and a half years in the Oklahoma House.

  • He describes himself as a crusader for the American farmer and says he has fought to protect Oklahoma values.
  • In the state House, his biography says, he fought to defend private-property rights and focused on agriculture.
  • In Congress he has helped write every farm bill since 1996 and serves on Agriculture and as a senior Republican on Financial Services.

District 4

Tom Cole

Republican

U.S. representative running for reelection in Oklahoma's 4th District

The campaign says Tom Cole has spent his career in service to Oklahoma. In Congress, it says, he has focused on national defense, taxpayers, small businesses, and Native American and tribal issues.

  • Announcing his 2026 reelection bid, Cole says a Republican Congress and White House have secured the border, brought inflation down, and cut taxes, including on tips, overtime, and Social Security.
  • He says Republicans passed energy legislation to create jobs in Oklahoma and raised military pay, expanded VA services, and increased law enforcement funding.
  • He says he is running to support America First policies, national security, and continued work at Fort Sill and Tinker Air Force Base.

Mitchell Jacob

Democratic

Army veteran, public defender, and Democrat running for Congress.

His campaign says he is a fourth-generation Oklahoman from Tulsa, a husband and father of five, and a former Army sergeant who served as a cryptologic linguist and deployed to Syria. He says he graduated from the University of Oklahoma College of Law in 2025 and works as a public defender.

  • He says public schools should be funded, serve free meals, and be the top priority, and that education is the greatest equalizer.
  • He says the national debt is the biggest threat to democracy and that Congress should stop raising the debt limit, cut spending, and eliminate the debt.
  • He says health care is a human right, that work visas and asylum claims should be processed fairly and quickly, and that he does not accept PAC money.

mitchelljacob.com

District 5

Stephanie Bice

Republican

U.S. representative and fourth-generation Oklahoman.

Her official biography says she was elected to Congress in 2020 after serving in the Oklahoma Senate from 2014 to 2020, and that she worked almost 20 years in business development, financial oversight, and sales. She earned a marketing degree from Oklahoma State University.

  • Her biography says that in the state legislature she focused on fiscal responsibility, economic development, and job growth.
  • In Congress she serves on Appropriations, including as vice chair of the Transportation, Housing, and Urban Development Subcommittee, and chairs a House Administration subcommittee on modernization and innovation.

bice.house.gov

Jena Nelson

Democratic

Educator and former Oklahoma Teacher of the Year running for Congress.

Her campaign says she taught for more than 16 years, was Oklahoma Teacher of the Year in 2020, and later worked in nonprofit and corporate leadership, including at the Oklahoma Faith Network. She says she and her husband Karl have two children, and that in 2022 she received more votes than any other Democrat in the race for state superintendent.

  • She says Social Security, Medicare, and Medicaid should be protected and strengthened, and that Medicare should negotiate lower drug prices.
  • She says a secure border and a fair immigration system both matter, with more agents and sensors against drug trafficking, faster asylum decisions, and more work visas.
  • She says she would expose backroom deals and opposes adding trillions to the deficit to pay for tax breaks for the wealthiest.
