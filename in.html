Secretary of State

The secretary of state runs the duties this state assigns that office. Those duties are not the same in every state. Indiana's secretary charters businesses, regulates securities, oversees elections, commissions notaries, registers trademarks, and licenses vehicle dealerships.

Max Engling

Republican

Republican nominee for Indiana secretary of state.

Max Engling says he worked through college in construction, earned a degree at IUPUI, and later a master's in national defense and strategic planning at the U.S. Naval War College. He describes work on the U.S. House Administration Committee, in House leadership, at an Indiana seed company, and most recently on the staff of U.S. Senator Jim Banks.

  • He says he will work so that only eligible citizens vote in Indiana and will advocate closed primaries.
  • He wants business filings screened for fraud, including shell companies, and he wants regulations on small businesses cut back.
  • He proposes a public portal where people can report possible election problems.

maxforindiana.com · Donate

Beau Bayh

Democratic

Marine infantry officer and attorney running for Indiana secretary of state.

Beau Bayh's campaign describes him as a seventh-generation Hoosier who served as a Marine infantry officer and rose to captain, including the Scout Sniper Unit Leader's Course.

  • He says his first priority is to stop waste and insider dealing in the secretary of state's office, including unnecessary travel, luxury vehicles, no-bid contracts, and high-paying jobs for family and friends.
  • He says illegal votes should not count and that every eligible Hoosier should be able to vote.
  • He supports statewide ballot referendums, including on redistricting, and says he will advocate for new small businesses and for working families.

beaubayh.com · Donate

Greg Ballard

Other

Former mayor of Indianapolis running for secretary of state as an independent.

Greg Ballard's site says he served 23 years as a Marine, including wartime service, then returned to Indianapolis, ran for mayor without establishment backing, and served two terms.

  • He says the secretary of state should run elections and should not endorse, raise money for, or donate to candidates who appear on those ballots.
  • He says he will not take sides in other state or local races.
  • He says the campaign is meant to give voters more choices at the ballot box.

gregballard.com · Donate

Lauri Shillings

Libertarian

Libertarian nominee for Indiana secretary of state.

Lauri Shillings's site says she is a creative director at the University of Indianapolis, has owned a small business since 2000, chairs the Hamilton County Libertarian Party, and holds a degree in community and nonprofit leadership.

  • She says she is running to restore trust in elections and to keep the office from being used as a party tool.
  • She wants simpler, reliable online services for business filings and records.
  • She says government systems should be efficient and that voters should have clear guides for registration and voting.

lauriforliberty.com

District 1

Barb Regnitz

Republican

Porter County commissioner running for Congress.

A Purdue graduate in mathematics and German, she worked as a software engineer and project manager at United Airlines and later as a financial advisor and certified financial planner. Elected to the Porter County Board of Commissioners in 2022, she says she knocked on more than 11,000 doors and has volunteered with Rotary and SCORE.

  • She says safe cities, secure borders, and smarter spending are her vision, including arresting and prosecuting criminals and ending catch-and-release.
  • She says she would curb inflation and waste so families keep more purchasing power, and reduce the national debt by putting American families and businesses ahead of foreign interests.
  • She says schools should teach students how to think, that girls' sports and facilities should be reserved for biological females, and that law-abiding people should keep the right to bear arms.

barbregnitzforcongress.com

Frank Mrvan

Democratic

U.S. representative for northwest Indiana, serving a third term.

A Morton High School and Ball State graduate, he and his wife Jane live in Lake County and have two daughters. Before Congress he was North Township Trustee for 15 years, and he says he is proud to represent Hammond, where he was born, and Highland, where he raised his family.

  • He says he supported the bipartisan infrastructure law and has delivered community-project funding for local infrastructure, the environment, first responders, and colleges, including work on the South Shore rail expansion.
  • He says the Inflation Reduction Act let Medicare negotiate drug prices, capped insulin at $35 a month for Medicare, and capped Medicare Part D out-of-pocket drug costs at $2,000.
  • He says he would ban stock trading by members of Congress, backs the Protecting the Right to Organize Act, and supports codifying Roe v. Wade.

  • He says he would protect Second Amendment rights and support veterans.

Republican

Businesswoman and U.S. representative for Indiana's 5th District.

Born in Ukraine, she says she immigrated after meeting her husband, became a U.S. citizen, and worked from bank teller to CPA, finance executive, and business owner. She served in the Indiana Senate and was elected to Congress in 2020.

  • She says the national debt should be cut by reducing spending and balancing the budget, not by raising taxes on citizens.

  • She says health-care costs need more transparency, competition, and consumer choice, and that politics should be a duty rather than a career.

Democratic

Indiana state senator running for Congress in the 5th District.

  • He says he would cap health-care and prescription costs and push corporations to account for prices on groceries, housing, and energy.
  • He says Washington needs a Democratic check so federal agencies are not above the law.

Republican

U.S. representative for Indiana's 6th District.

His office says he founded Storage Express, grew it for three decades, and merged it into a company that trades on the New York Stock Exchange. He co-owned a software firm, invested in oil and gas wells and in farm and ranch land, and served two terms on the Indianapolis City-County Council. He sits on the House Appropriations Committee. He graduated from Indiana University, earned a master's from the University of London, and an MBA in agribusiness from Purdue. He and his wife, Mary, live on Indianapolis's south side.

  • His office says he wants policy that encourages economic development, and that he is focused on reining in federal spending and the national debt.
  • His office says he is working to advance border security and to safeguard conservative Hoosier values.
  • His campaign lists making life more affordable, supporting farmers and small businesses, backing law enforcement and the military, protecting the unborn, and defending the Second Amendment.
