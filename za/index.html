South Africa · Local government elections · Wednesday, 4 November 2026

South Africa is electing local councils.

This is not an election for president, and it is not the national parliament. Voters are choosing the councils that run cities, towns, and rural areas.

The country is divided into 213 municipalities. A municipality is the area one local council governs. Johannesburg and Cape Town are municipalities. So is a rural council whose name you may not know. A province is one of South Africa's nine larger regions, the level above a municipality.

The map is those municipalities. Beige means the Electoral Commission has not reported votes there. A color appears only when reported votes have one party ahead. That color is the one the Commission records for the party. It does not stand for a party from another country. This map does not declare a winner.

A ward is a smaller piece inside a municipality, closer to a neighborhood or a city district. Ward lines are not drawn. A ward would be colored only from that ward's own reported votes, and those outlines are not matched to this election's file.

The Commission publishes voting stations on its own site. This page does not ask for a home address.

Leaders.vote is an independent voter guide. It is not a campaign, not an endorsement, and not a site of any election authority. This page is South Africa.

Vote totals are copied from the Electoral Commission results file for the 4 November 2026 local government elections. A place is colored only when that file reports votes and one party is ahead. This site does not call a race. It does not ask for a home address, a phone number, or an email address.

Municipality outlines are the 2020 local-municipality boundaries published by geoBoundaries from the Municipal Demarcation Board, under CC BY 3.0 IGO. A reported total that does not match one outline is listed and left off the map.
