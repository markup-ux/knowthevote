State · Vote for one.

Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials. Ohio's official ballot prints the governor and lieutenant governor as one choice.

Vivek Ramaswamy

Republican

Cincinnati native and business leader running for governor.

His campaign describes him as a husband and father who has spent his career turning business ideas into results. He says he is running to restore opportunity and keep the American Dream available in Ohio.

  • He says the state should be more affordable, with lower taxes, lower energy bills, and more money in household budgets.
  • He says children should get a world-class education.
  • He says the state should protect neighborhoods, revive cities, and put unity ahead of division.

vivekforohio.com · Donate

Amy Acton

Democratic

Physician running for governor.

Her campaign says she grew up in Youngstown under difficult circumstances and is a doctor, advocate, and problem-solver. She says she is running because families are struggling while wealthy interests pull the state in another direction.

  • The site lists lower household costs, health-care costs, education, jobs, worker protections, safer communities, and corruption as her priorities.
  • A campaign update says medical debt should be eliminated and health care made more affordable.
  • She also lists reproductive freedom and the ability to age in place among the issues on the site.

actonforgovernor.com · Donate

Don Kissick

Libertarian

Libertarian candidate for governor on the Kissick/Mills ticket.

The campaign site says the ticket is running because its candidates were tired of choosing between the two major parties. It says it wants a political home for Ohioans who feel they do not have one.

  • The site says too many institutions have become corrupt, self-serving, and comfortable expanding power without representing Ohioans.
  • It says liberty, kindness, justice, common sense, and integrity should be the test for every bill and political leader.
  • It says the campaign will not support a policy that asks voters to give those principles up.

Sherrod Brown

Democratic

Candidate for U.S. Senate who says he was born and raised in Mansfield.

His campaign says he saw corporations seek tax breaks and trade deals that sent Ohio jobs overseas, and that he has opposed those deals. It says he lives in Columbus with his wife, Connie Schultz.

  • He says he led the effort to restore pensions after Wall Street losses, including retirement payments for more than 100,000 Ohioans.
  • He says he fought drug-company prices, including a cap on insulin and lower prices on other prescriptions for seniors.
  • He says he worked to get care for veterans exposed to burn pits and to restore full Social Security payments for former public workers.

State · Vote for one.

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law.

Keith Faber

Republican

Ohio auditor of state running for attorney general.

His campaign says he was elected auditor after more than two decades that included private-sector work and public office, and that he keeps a law practice in Celina. It says his audit team has pursued criminal cases against public officials and findings to recover taxpayer money.

  • He says he would defend constitutional rights, including religious liberty, and challenge federal actions he argues exceed federal authority.
  • He says he would fight the drug epidemic with treatment, prosecutions, and efforts to cut supply chains, and that he would work with police on human trafficking and survivor services.
  • He says he would enforce consumer-protection laws against fraud, scams, and deceptive businesses, especially cases that target seniors, and he proposes a program to help patients challenge health-insurance denials.

keithfaber.org · Donate

John Kulewicz

Democratic

Columbus lawyer and Upper Arlington city council member running for attorney general.

His campaign says he grew up in Columbus, studied at Ohio State, and then spent more than 40 years as a litigator after a clerkship. It says he has been on the Upper Arlington council since 2020 and has won cases in the U.S. Supreme Court and Ohio courts.

  • He says the attorney general should be independent of Columbus politics and should work for the people who pay the office.
  • He says he would take on corrupt politicians and restore transparency and accountability.
  • He says he would pursue price-fixing, scams, robocalls, and fraud by Medicaid providers and nursing homes.

john4ohioag.com

State · Vote for one.

Secretary of State

Ohio law makes the secretary of state the chief election officer. The office appoints the members of county boards of elections, prescribes ballot forms, and certifies the names of candidates for state offices.

Robert Sprague

Republican

State treasurer running for secretary of state.

His campaign says his background runs from engineering to finance and public policy. It says that as treasurer he invested taxpayer money, cut waste, and modernized systems.

  • He says he is running to defend the vote, the voter's voice, and the family.
  • He says he wants the office to run the most secure elections in the state's history.

spragueforohio.com · Donate

Allison Russo

Democratic

Candidate for Ohio secretary of state.

Her campaign says she is running because every voice matters and every vote counts. She says politicians in Columbus and Washington are trying to hold onto power, and that the power belongs with Ohioans.

  • She says the secretary of state should protect each voter's voice and keep elections fair, secure, and accessible.
  • She says voters should choose their politicians, and that she has fought officials who rig the system to protect their own power.
  • She says she would restore trust in elections, protect personal data, and make government answerable on costs and the other problems families face.

allisonrusso.com · Donate
