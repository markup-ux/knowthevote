Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials. The governor enforces laws and maintains peace, and serves as commander in chief of Georgia's military forces, including the Georgia National Guard when they are under state authority.

Rick Jackson

Republican

Founder of Jackson Healthcare, running for governor.

His letter says he built Jackson Healthcare, which operates in all 50 states, helps care for more than 20 million patients a year, and generates over $3 billion in annual revenue. He describes a childhood in public housing and five foster homes, raised by a mother struggling with alcoholism.

  • He says he is running to cut the income tax, freeze property taxes, and lower costs so Georgia is the most affordable state.
  • He says he stepped in when China threatened the U.S. antibiotic supply, and that Governor Brian Kemp called him during COVID to help supply doctors and nurses.
  • He says he will also work to secure the state.

Former Atlanta mayor running for governor.

She was Atlanta's 60th mayor and says she is the first mayor in the city's history to have served in all three branches of city government, as a judge, a city council member, and mayor. She says the city balanced four budgets without a property-tax increase, layoffs, or furloughs, and that reserves stood near $181 million at the end of her term.

  • She says that as mayor she secured major investments in affordable housing and a historic pay raise for Atlanta's first responders.
  • She says she supported small businesses and became a national voice on civil-rights reforms.
  • She says the city balanced its budgets through a downturn without property-tax increases, layoffs, or furloughs.

Lt. Governor

The lieutenant governor is next in line if the governor cannot finish the term. Any other duties are set by state law. As president of the Senate, the lieutenant governor presides over debate in the Senate chamber.

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law. In Georgia, the office gives legal opinions that bind state agencies and prosecutes public-corruption cases involving dealings with the state.

Secretary of State

The secretary of state runs the duties this state assigns that office. Those duties are not the same in every state. Georgia's secretary of state is responsible for administering elections, registering corporations, regulating charities and securities, and overseeing professional license holders.

Republican

State representative, former deputy secretary of state, and former chief of staff to the governor.

He says that as deputy secretary of state under Brian Kemp he managed a $30 million budget and about 250 employees. He later served as Governor Kemp's chief of staff, overseeing day-to-day agency operations and helping manage a budget he puts near $60 billion, and he now represents House District 114.

  • He says elections should be secure, accessible, fair, and trusted, and he says he will protect voter ID and election transparency.
  • He says he reformed professional licensing so a new license could be issued in five days and renewals became automatic.
  • He says he will cut red tape for small businesses, improve customer service, and use technology so state government works for Georgians.
