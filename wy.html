Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials. Wyoming's constitution makes the governor commander-in-chief of the state's military forces and charges the governor with taking care that the laws are faithfully executed.

Eric Barlow

Republican

Rancher, large-animal veterinarian, and Marine veteran who served as speaker of the Wyoming House and then in the State Senate.

His campaign says he is a fourth-generation Wyomingite raised on a Campbell County ranch who returned after the Marines, earned a veterinary degree, and kept the family ranch with his wife, Kelly.

  • He says his legislative work has centered on Wyoming energy independence, rural health access, and water infrastructure.
  • He says he is running to offer leadership rooted in service, and that the campaign is counting on Wyoming residents rather than special-interest money.

Kenneth Casner

Democratic

Third-generation Wyomingite and Vietnam veteran running for governor.

He told Wyoming Public Media he was born in Cheyenne in 1950, that his family has worked in Wyoming for three generations, and that he ran a small business in Elk Mountain for 28 years. He does not have a campaign website.

  • He says his first issue is free and open elections: a person should be able to file and run without having to join a party.
  • He says health care is the main reason young people leave, and he wants a statewide system that connects rural clinics.
  • He says he wants small business to grow, and he wants property tax ended for people once they turn 65.

Rebecca Bextel

Other

Small-business owner running for governor of Wyoming.

Her campaign says she has lived in Wyoming for more than 20 years, raised a family and run small businesses with her husband, Jonathan, and served as a Republican precinct committeewoman, state committeewoman, and an alternate delegate to the 2020 and 2024 Republican National Conventions.

  • She says she would defend gun rights, religious liberty, private property, and parents' authority over their children's education, and veto bills she says infringe on those freedoms.
  • She says she would take Wyoming out of the federal refugee resettlement program and end policies that let people without legal status get a Wyoming driver's license.
  • She says she would defend coal, oil, natural gas, uranium, and critical-mineral jobs, and fight property-tax bills that rise faster than a family can pay.

Senate

The winner is one of 100 votes on federal law, spending, treaties, and judges. The term is six years. The U.S. Senate says it alone conducts impeachment trials and must confirm ambassadors, judges, and other officers the president nominates.

Harriet Hageman

Republican

Member of Congress and lawyer whose campaign says she has fought federal regulators in court.

Her campaign says she has spent her career challenging rules that affect ranchers, farmers, and energy producers, and that Congress should take back power it has given to federal agencies.

  • She says she will defend speech, gun rights, and private property, and that she wants voter identification and an end to mailing ballots to people who did not request them.
  • She says she will fight tax increases, cut federal spending, and defend oil, gas, and coal production.
  • She says federal land agencies have mismanaged forests and that she wants multiple-use rules that answer to the people who live with those decisions.

hagemanforwyoming.com · Donate

James Byrd

Democratic

Wyoming legislator running for the U.S. Senate.

His campaign says he is a native of Wyoming whose family roots in the state go back to homesteading in 1862, and that he has worked across the aisle in the Legislature.

  • He says he will try to lower energy costs by backing domestic production, cutting red tape, and ending foreign wars he says are driving prices up.
  • He says he will keep public lands public and open for hunting, fishing, grazing, and recreation, rather than selling them.

Secretary of State

The secretary of state runs the duties this state assigns that office. Those duties are not the same in every state. Wyoming's secretary of state says the office keeps the Great Seal and is where businesses, elections, and securities filings are recorded.

Robert Short

Republican

Businessman and former election judge running for Wyoming secretary of state.

His campaign says the office has to follow rules that build trust, fairness, and accuracy, and that he would carry out the constitutional and statutory duties of the job.

  • He says he would use the office against sham corporations and people who misuse privacy laws to disadvantage Wyoming businesses.
  • He says fair elections depend on county clerks and local volunteers, and that he would act quickly if the process goes wrong.
  • He says he would investigate securities violations, including cryptocurrency scams, and refer criminal matters to the proper authorities.

Tamara Trujillo

Other

Former Wyoming House member running for secretary of state.

She told Wyoming Public Media she represented House District 44 and spent 17 years as an administrator for a large oil company in the state, writing policy and tightening how the company worked. She is the Constitution Party nominee and says that party fits her Christian and conservative views on what government should do.

  • She says elections should be secure and open to U.S. citizens, and that county clerks need the tools and a shared system to do that work.
  • She supports hand counts as a second check on machine totals and supports mail ballots. She opposes ballot drop boxes and absentee voting for anyone who asks.
  • She says Wyoming's easy business filings bring in money but also fraud, and she wants a company's owners identified when it registers.

Chuck Gray

Republican

Wyoming secretary of state and candidate for Congress

The campaign says Chuck Gray served six years in the Wyoming House before becoming secretary of state, an office that also serves as lieutenant governor. It says he has focused on election administration, energy, and abortion limits.

  • His campaign says he will protect coal, oil, and gas jobs, oppose what he calls woke wind projects and ESG rules, and fight inflation with lower taxes and less spending.
  • The site says he sponsored Wyoming's voter ID law, required proof of citizenship to register, and banned ranked-choice voting and foreign funding of elections.
  • It says he supports securing the border, the Second Amendment, and pro-life policies, and opposes taxpayer-funded abortion.

Lisa Kinney

Democratic

Former Wyoming state senator running for Congress.

Her campaign says she was born and raised in Laramie, earned degrees from the University of Wyoming and the University of Oregon and a law degree from Wyoming, and served in the State Senate from Laramie for ten years, from 1985 to 1995, including as minority floor leader. She says she has directed the Albany County Library, practiced law, and worked more than twenty years as a financial advisor.

  • She says public lands should stay public, and she would work with local communities to keep access open rather than sell or transfer the land.
  • She says she would restore Affordable Care Act subsidies, keep the rural-hospital funding she says is set to end, and work toward health coverage for everyone.
  • She says she would cut tariffs, raise taxes on the wealthy, lower taxes for people making under $100,000, and help first-time buyers and renters with housing.
