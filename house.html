Blue slips

A blue slip is the paper the Senate Judiciary Committee sends to the two senators from a nominee's home state. The president nominates the judge. Those senators return the paper if they will let the committee take up that nomination. For a district court, the committee does not hold a hearing unless both senators return the slip. That returned paper is what assigns the judge a hearing. A circuit nomination can be heard without the slips. The committee does not publish each paper. A hearing, a place on the Executive Calendar, or a confirmation is the public sign that both district-court slips came back. A deeper blue means both slips came back. A paler blue means the return is not on this record. An outline means a circuit nomination. Blue is this paper only. It is not a party, and it is not a yes or a no.
