Federal · Vote for one

Senate

The winner is one of 100 votes on federal law, spending, treaties, and judges. The term is six years. The Senate has the sole power to try impeachments.

Andy Barr

Republican

U.S. representative from Kentucky running for the Senate.

Andy Barr's campaign calls him an eighth-generation Kentuckian and says he was the first member of the state's House delegation to endorse Donald Trump and chaired Trump's 2024 Kentucky primary campaign.

  • He says he would deport people he calls criminal illegal immigrants, restart border-wall construction, hire more border agents, restore Remain in Mexico, and end catch-and-release.
  • He says he voted for tax cuts that end taxes on Social Security for seniors, on overtime, and on tips, and he wants to expand Kentucky coal and block climate rules he says were imposed through financial regulators.
  • He says he will defend girls' sports and private spaces, veterans' disability and health benefits, the Second Amendment, police funding, sanctions on fentanyl tied to China, and Kentucky farmers, including a federal framework for hemp products.

barrforsenate.com · Donate

Charles Booker

Democratic

Former Kentucky state representative running for the U.S. Senate.

Charles Booker's site says he is a lifelong Kentuckian and the son of a West Louisville family, and that he grew up rationing insulin. He says he built a campaign that reaches from the city to the hollers around health care, jobs, and leaders who fight for people instead of corporations.

  • He says Kentucky needs a senator who treats poverty as an emergency, because families can be one medical bill, layoff, or storm from losing everything.
  • He says health care is a human right and that he wants union jobs in every county.
  • He also calls for clean water, clean air, and a politics grounded in the dignity of the people it serves.

charlesbooker.org · Donate

Christopher Campbell

Other

Kentucky Party candidate for U.S. Senate.

Christopher Campbell's site says he is a dues-paying member of a labor union, served in the Kentucky National Guard, and is a Kentucky Colonel.

  • He supports Medicare for All and says the federal government should provide basic medical care.
  • He says workers need safe conditions, strong benefits, good pay, and a way to raise grievances without retaliation.
  • He wants a constitutional environmental amendment and a ban on carbon credits that he says let facilities keep polluting.

Every district NBC News is publishing for this state. This list is not filtered to one address. A written note is added only from that candidate's own site.

District 1

James Comer

Republican

U.S. representative and chair of the House Oversight Committee.

He grew up in rural Monroe County, studied agriculture at Western Kentucky University, and bought his first farm after graduating in 1993. He served six terms in the Kentucky House, was state agriculture commissioner, and was elected to Congress in 2016.

  • He says that as Oversight chair he is leading major investigations and pushing to reduce government waste, fraud, and abuse.
  • He says he wants a balanced federal budget, rebuilt infrastructure, and business-friendly policies that create opportunity for families.
  • He says his record includes work on the 2018 farm bill and legislation to lift regulations and increase government accountability.

jamescomer.com

John Williams

Democratic

Western Kentucky boat-dock builder running for Congress.

He says he has spent his life in western Kentucky, graduated from Murray State in 2016 with a history degree, and has built boat docks on Kentucky Lake and Lake Barkley since he was 14 while working in the family business. He says neighbors and elders shaped him and that he is running to take those values to Washington.

  • The campaign says it is grassroots and does not take corporate money.
  • His site says he stands for farm resilience, congressional accountability, workplace dignity, fairer drug prices, and veterans' services.
  • He also lists infrastructure, data-center rules, child care, tax policy for everyday Americans, and health-insurance reform among the issues he is running on.

williamsforky.com · Donate

District 2

Brett Guthrie

Republican

U.S. representative and chair of the House Energy and Commerce Committee.

After Army service he joined a Bowling Green manufacturing business his father started, represented Kentucky's 32nd Senate district, and was elected to the U.S. House in 2008. He says representing the Second District is the honor of a lifetime.

  • He says he is focused on lower health-care and prescription costs, energy independence, and cutting taxes and regulations.
  • He says he wants to protect seniors, strengthen domestic manufacturing, and keep government out of the doctor-patient relationship.
  • The campaign says he is pro-life, has an A rating from the NRA, and has been called a friend of the Farm Bureau.

brettguthrie.com · Donate

Megan Wingfield

Democratic

Mechanic and community advocate from Bowling Green running for Congress.

Born and raised in Bowling Green, she says she earned an automotive technology degree at Southcentral Kentucky Community and Technical College and works as a mechanic. She says she is also a caregiver and activist running because the district needs someone who knows what it is to struggle.

  • She says one job should support a family, workers need a living wage, and health care should be universal so illness does not cause bankruptcy.
  • She says she would protect reproductive freedom, support universal pre-K and affordable child care, and expand VA care, mental-health services, and housing for veterans.
  • She says she signed a term-limits pledge and wants independent mechanics to have access to vehicle diagnostics that manufacturers now lock behind paywalls.

wingfieldforcongress.com · Donate

District 3

Maria Rodriguez

Republican

Louisville trauma nurse and small-business owner running for Congress.

Born in Cuba, she says she arrived in the United States at 21 after an 11-year asylum process, became a citizen, built a small business, and later earned a nursing degree as a single mother of two. She says she is running to defend freedoms she once lived without.

  • She says she believes in free enterprise, safe communities, legal immigration, and constitutional rights.
  • Her campaign lists defending capitalism, a safer Louisville, lower taxes, and protecting the unborn among her priorities.
  • She also lists protecting women's sports.

mariaforuscongress.com

Morgan McGarvey

Democratic

U.S. representative for Louisville and Kentucky's only Democrat in Congress.

First elected in 2022, he says he serves on Veterans Affairs, Small Business, and Budget, and that he was minority leader in the Kentucky Senate for part of a decade before Congress. He says public schools in Louisville opened possibilities he wants every child to have.

  • He says health care is a human right and that he supports Medicare for All, Medicare drug-price negotiation, and coverage for vision, dental, and hearing.
  • He says he has brought home federal funding, including money for Simmons College, and that his office has returned Social Security, veterans' benefits, and tax refunds to families.
  • He says he passed a House bill to protect veterans' benefits and is pushing a nationwide ban on no-knock search warrants in Breonna Taylor's name.

morganmcgarvey.com · Donate

District 4

Ed Gallrein

Republican

Navy SEAL, farmer, and Trump-endorsed candidate for Congress.

A fifth-generation Kentucky farmer, he says he served three decades as a Navy SEAL officer, rising to captain, including SEAL Team Six, and earned three Bronze Stars. He says he returned home to keep farming and support veterans.

  • He says he would cut taxes and regulations, stop spending he blames for inflation, and bring jobs back to Kentucky.
  • He says he would finish a border wall, fund Border Patrol and ICE, and rebuild the military.
  • He says he would defend gun rights without compromise, that he is pro-life, and that he would eliminate DEI programs in schools, the military, and government.

edgallrein.com · Donate

Melissa Strange

Democratic

Supply-chain director from northern Kentucky running for Congress.

A lifelong northern Kentuckian from Villa Hills, she says she earned degrees at Northern Kentucky University and Thomas More while working, and has spent about 27 years in food manufacturing and supply chain. She and her husband Andy live in Erlanger and have two sons.

  • She says she would protect and expand Medicare, Medicaid, and Social Security, lower drug costs, and make treatment easier to find than a dealer.
  • She says she takes no corporate or foreign PAC money and supports term limits, a ban on congressional stock trading, and an end to Citizens United.
  • She says data centers should need community approval, and she wants the government out of reproductive health care and a right to repair farm and consumer equipment.

strangeforky.com · Donate

District 5

Hal Rogers

Republican

Dean of the U.S. House, representing southern and eastern Kentucky since 1981.

Born in rural Kentucky, he says he has been elected to 23 consecutive terms and became the longest-serving House member in 2022. He points to Operation UNITE, the Center for Rural Development, and SOAR as regional projects he helped build.

  • He says that as Appropriations chair from 2011 to 2016 he cut discretionary spending by $126 billion and pushed oversight of federal agencies.
  • He says he led the first Homeland Security appropriations subcommittee and pressed for immigration enforcement and border security.
  • After winning the 2026 primary he said he would keep fighting for conservative values, jobs at home, and energy independence.

congressmanhalrogers.com · Donate

Ned Pillersdorf

Democratic

Poverty-law lawyer running for Congress in eastern Kentucky.

He says he has had an underdog, high-volume poverty law practice for 44 years and has worked with several nonprofits. He announced on the Fourth of July because he says he fears for vulnerable neighbors in Appalachia.

  • He says he opposes cuts to Medicaid, Medicare, food stamps, utility aid, public media, Job Corps, and cancer research that he says fund tax breaks for billionaires.
  • He says the incumbent's vote for the tax-and-spending bill betrayed a district that depends on rural hospitals and the Affordable Care Act.
  • He says he will campaign on health-care access, better internet, education, and clean drinking water, and that he supports uncapping the House and term limits.

nedforcongress.com · Donate

District 6

Ralph Alvarado

Republican

Physician running for Congress in Kentucky's Sixth District.

He says he is a Christian, husband, father, and doctor who has cared for patients across central Kentucky for nearly three decades. He says he is running to bring independent leadership, lower the cost of living, and strengthen the state's economy.

  • He says he will work with both parties on affordable health care, lower costs, more transparency, and more patient control.
  • He says he wants groceries, gas, and health care to cost less, good-paying jobs to grow, and wasteful spending that feeds inflation to end.
  • His campaign says he would toughen punishments for Medicare and Medicaid fraud and make big insurers disclose profits, denial rates, and wait times.

alvaradoforcongress.com · Donate

Zach Dembo

Democratic

Former federal prosecutor, Navy JAG officer, and teacher running for Congress.

Raised in Lexington, he says he is a ninth-generation Kentuckian who taught eighth grade in the Mississippi Delta, served more than four years as a Navy lawyer, and later prosecuted civil-rights cases and advised Governor Andy Beshear. He says he resigned as a prosecutor because he believed the Justice Department had been politicized.

  • He says tariffs are taxes crushing small businesses and that full-time work should cover a family's bills, including a living wage of at least $15 an hour.
  • He says Medicaid cuts hurt families and hospitals, that one in three Kentuckians relies on Medicaid, and that women should make their own health-care decisions.
  • He says data centers are driving up energy bills and taking farmland without enough local say, and that he left the Justice Department to fight corruption.
