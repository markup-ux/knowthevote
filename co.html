Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials.

Victor Marx

Republican

Candidate for governor of Colorado.

Victor Marx says he is a husband, father, and community worker who has supported veterans and people in crisis, and that he founded organizations to do that work. He says he is running to restore trust and put families' safety and costs first.

  • He says he will back law enforcement and make public safety a first priority.
  • He says he will lower the cost of living, change housing policy, cut taxes, and push for good-paying jobs.
  • He says government should answer to taxpayers rather than to special interests.

Phil Weiser

Democratic

Colorado attorney general running for governor.

Phil Weiser, the attorney general, says he is the son and grandson of Holocaust survivors and a first-generation American. He says he clerked at the U.S. Supreme Court, taught at the University of Colorado, and is running to keep the state safer and more affordable.

  • He says he will work with local leaders to make housing more affordable and to let more people live near their jobs.
  • He says he will fight to keep health coverage affordable and to protect the care Coloradans already use.
  • He says he will work with law enforcement, fire chiefs, and victims' advocates on safety at home, at school, and in the community.

Greg Lopez

Other

Air Force veteran and former Parker mayor running for governor.

Greg Lopez, a U.S. Air Force veteran and former mayor and city manager of Parker, says state government should live within a budget the way families do.

  • He says he will use zero-based budgeting and review agencies for waste, duplication, and rules that do not help people.
  • He says he will push for 25,000 modest starter homes in four years and for lower water and sewer connection fees.
  • He says he will hold the transportation department to congestion relief and road repair, and expand school choice and vocational training.

Stephen Hamilton

Other

Small-business owner running for governor of Colorado.

He told the Fort Collins Coloradoan he runs Scooter's Autoservice, a towing and recovery business. He is the American Constitution Party nominee. He does not have a campaign website and points voters to a Facebook page.

  • He says he would make state spending more transparent, cut waste, and pursue property and tax relief.
  • He says he would rebuild mining, manufacturing, agriculture, transportation, and energy jobs while protecting land and water.
  • He says he would lower the burden on working Coloradans and put their interests ahead of parties and special interests.

Jeff Peckman

Other

Unity Party candidate for governor.

Jeff Peckman says he is running for governor with T.J. Cole, chair of the Unity Party of Colorado, as his running mate. He says his own campaign is self-funded and is not taking donations.

  • He says Colorado companies can cut air pollution without new electric-vehicle mandates, and that he would declare an air-pollution emergency on his first day.
  • He says he would create a chief coherence officer and a partnership to launch new technology projects.
  • He says he will work to end TABOR, which he calls a limit that is hurting the state.

Candidate for governor of Colorado.

Erik Underwood says he has fought corporate fraud, including eight years in federal court against Bank of America, and that he holds a patent on an artificial-intelligence product. He says he would bring that same fight to the governor's office.

Eric Mulder

Libertarian

Libertarian nominee for governor of Colorado.

Eric Mulder says he is a U.S. Army veteran who served in Afghanistan as a chaplain's assistant. He says that work showed him people from very different backgrounds can pull together when the mission comes first, and that Colorado should govern in that spirit.

  • He says programs should be judged by the results they deliver, not by how many dollars they spend, and that communities closest to a problem should be free to solve it.
  • He proposes a one-year break from the state sales tax for places in economic distress, with local voters deciding whether to replace it with a local tax for schools, roads, water, emergency services, or other needs.
  • He says he would oppose government cameras that track every passing car, and he wants the state to clear a path for small nuclear reactors, including reactors that large power users could run themselves.

Senate

The winner is one of 100 votes on federal law, spending, treaties, and judges. The term is six years.

Mark Baisley

Republican

Colorado state senator and Republican nominee for U.S. Senate.

Mark Baisley says he is an aerospace engineer who leads a Colorado software company and has served in the state House and the state Senate. He says he is the Republican nominee for the U.S. Senate in 2026.

  • He says that in the legislature he worked on jobs, education, and essential services while limiting government intrusion into private life.
  • He says he has defended water rights, protected the energy industry, and opposed reckless spending and government overreach.
  • He says he will take that approach to Washington, with an emphasis on liberty and Colorado families.

baisleyforsenate.com

John Hickenlooper

Democratic

U.S. senator and former Colorado governor.

John Hickenlooper's campaign describes him as a former brewpub owner, a former governor, and a sitting senator. It says he is the only peer-reviewed scientist in the Senate.

  • He says he takes no corporate PAC money and supports overturning Citizens United, tighter disclosure of political money, and protection of vote-by-mail.
  • He says he is working on housing costs, small-business capital, and union rights, including the PRO Act.
  • He says health care is a right, and that he supports a public option, Medicare negotiation of drug prices, and restoring Roe v. Wade.

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law.

Michael Allen

Republican

District attorney running for Colorado attorney general.

Michael Allen says he is the elected district attorney for El Paso and Teller counties, a former Navy aviation electronics technician, and a husband and father. He says he has worked as a prosecutor since a law-school internship in 2004.

  • He says he will put victims first, use the statewide grand jury on crimes that cross county lines, and work with sheriffs and police across the state, including rural departments.
  • He says he will review the attorney general's spending, cut waste, and treat crime as a driver of insurance and business costs.
  • He says he will investigate public corruption without regard to party and will defend Colorado's water rights in disputes with other states.

michaelallenforcolorado.com

Jena Griswold

Democratic

Colorado secretary of state running for attorney general.

Jena Griswold says she grew up in a working-class family in Estes Park, was the first in her family to attend a four-year college, and earned a law degree. She says she has been secretary of state since 2018 and is running for attorney general to stand up to Donald Trump and protect Coloradans' rights.

  • She says she will defend the rights and freedoms of Coloradans and will not be intimidated.
  • She says that as secretary of state she backed laws against threats to election workers, guns near voting centers, and tampering with voting equipment.
  • She says she expanded ballot drop boxes, in-person voting, campus and tribal voting access, and automatic voter registration.

Secretary of State

The secretary of state runs the duties this state assigns that office. Those duties are not the same in every state.

James Wiley

Republican

Republican nominee for Colorado secretary of state.

James Wiley says he is a fifth-generation Coloradan, born in Colorado Springs, who lived in Kazakhstan as a child and later earned a management degree and an MBA from CSU Pueblo. He says he is the Republican nominee and calls himself the Red Flame of Liberty.

  • He says elections should be held on one day, in person, with voter identification, paper ballots, and citizen hand counts.
  • He says he would put public records, including voter rolls and business filings, on a blockchain he calls Liberty Chain.
  • He says he would cut red tape and fees so it is faster and cheaper to start a business.

Jefferson County clerk and recorder running for secretary of state.

Amanda Gonzalez says she is Jefferson County's clerk and recorder, an attorney, and the Democratic nominee for secretary of state. She says she previously led Colorado Common Cause and worked on voting rights and the state's independent redistricting commission.

  • She says that as clerk she has worked to expand ballot access, improve election security, and run elections in one of the state's largest counties.
  • She says the office should serve Colorado, not her own politics.
  • She says she will work so every voice is heard, every vote is counted, and elections stay secure.
