Senate

The winner is one of 100 votes on federal law, spending, treaties, and judges. The term is six years. The U.S. Senate has the sole power to conduct impeachment trials.

Michael Whatley

Republican

Republican candidate for U.S. Senate.

His campaign says he has spent his career fighting for the country and for North Carolina's conservative values. He says he is running to take that fight to Washington and to put North Carolina first.

  • He says he is running to fight for secure borders.
  • He says he wants strong leadership and what he calls North Carolina values.
  • He asks voters to stand with him and with President Trump.

votemichaelwhatley.com

Roy Cooper

Democratic

Former governor and former attorney general of North Carolina.

His campaign says he was born and raised in Nash County, practiced law in Rocky Mount, served in the legislature, and was attorney general for four terms before he was elected governor. He says that as governor he worked with both parties on jobs and on a Medicaid expansion.

  • He says he is running to lower the cost of food, health care, energy, child care, and housing.
  • He says the middle class feels out of reach while the largest corporations and the richest Americans have gained wealth.
  • He says that as attorney general he prosecuted crimes and went after scammers, predatory lenders, drug companies, and big banks.

Retired public school teacher.

His campaign says he is a native North Carolinian, a recently retired teacher, and a community activist who has led a prison ministry for more than twenty years. He says he is a veteran and that this campaign refuses corporate PAC money.

S

Shannon Bray

Libertarian

Navy veteran and cybersecurity professional running for the U.S. Senate.

His campaign describes a volunteer-led effort, built county by county, centered on privacy, accountability, and government that works. He says rights should hold as technology advances, and that security should not depend on a surveillance state.

  • He calls for lower costs, simpler taxes, and an end to wasteful spending, and he says inflation should not quietly drain people's savings.
  • He argues people should own their data, with consent that is specific and revocable, and he says he will defend privacy, free speech, and due process.
  • He also wants secure borders with a workable legal process, and elections that are secure and auditable.

District 2

Eugene Douglass

Republican

Republican nominee for North Carolina's 2nd District.

His campaign brochure says he was raised in Swarthmore, Pennsylvania, earned a PhD in fiber and polymer science at North Carolina State and a master of divinity, and taught college chemistry before retiring as a chemistry and education consultant. He says he moved to Wake County in 2023 and is running to replace Deborah Ross.

  • He proposes a flat tax with minimal deductions for people and businesses, and less federal regulation so states handle more local affairs.
  • He says human life should be protected from conception to natural death, and he calls for a ban on partial-birth abortion and for states to limit what he describes as gender surgeries on children and euthanasia.
  • He wants elementary and secondary education controlled by states and localities, with the focus on reading, writing, science, and math.
