District 1

Paul Berry III

Republican

Candidate for Missouri's 1st Congressional District.

His campaign site is a letter to Missouri voters about the 2026 race in the 1st District, which he describes as the City of St. Louis and north St. Louis County. He says he has been traveling the state to organize support.

  • He says he refuses to surrender St. Louis to officials associated with the Democratic Socialists of America, which he calls a foreign ideology that puts government in charge of what people earn and keep.
  • He says public policy in St. Louis has bred murder, failures in education, and economic despair, and he wants those failures put in front of voters.
  • He asks county parties and grassroots groups to phone-bank and write letters to 1st District voters through Election Day.

Wesley Bell

Democratic

U.S. representative for Missouri's 1st District.

His campaign says the job is to bring federal results home to St. Louis, and it points to infrastructure, public safety, workforce, and neighborhood projects he pursued while Republicans controlled Congress and the White House.

  • He says every family in St. Louis should have a safe, affordable home, and he highlights federal housing grants meant to add supply and lower costs for renters and buyers.
  • He wants Lambert Airport modernized so flights stay on schedule and the region stays connected.
  • He says he fought to expand the Radiation Exposure Compensation Act for North County families sickened by radioactive contamination at Coldwater Creek.

District 2

Ann Wagner

Republican

U.S. representative for Missouri's 2nd District.

She grew up working at her parents' carpet store in Manchester, studied business at the University of Missouri, and later held management jobs at Hallmark and Ralston Purina before coming to Congress.

  • She says she has worked to cut taxes and regulation so local businesses can hire, and she voted for a tax bill that removes taxes on tips, overtime, and Social Security.
  • She highlights the Fight Online Sex Trafficking Act and funding for rape-kit testing through the Debbie Smith Act.
  • She says she will keep defending a right to life, and she introduced a bill to require medical care for infants born alive after an abortion.

Fred Wellman

Democratic

Retired Army veteran running for Congress in Missouri.

His campaign says he served 22 years and four combat tours, then spent a decade in Washington advocating for veterans.

  • He says housing, health care, child care, and groceries are outrunning wages, and he wants lower costs, higher pay, and the right to organize, including a higher federal minimum wage and paid family leave.
  • On health care he supports restoring ACA subsidies, reversing Medicaid cuts, a public option, and a lower Medicare eligibility age, with universal coverage as the longer-term goal.
  • He says Social Security, Medicare, and pensions should be protected so people can retire without choosing between medicine, rent, and heat.

Ballot measures.

The title is the one being published for this ballot. This guide adds an explanation only when it comes from the official text.

Amendment 3: Repeal abortion rights and prohibit gender transition

A yes vote would repeal the 2024 constitutional right to reproductive care, including abortion through fetal viability, permit limits tied to medical emergencies, rape and incest under twelve weeks, and fetal anomalies, and prohibit gender-transition surgeries, cross-sex hormones, and puberty blockers for minors except for specific medical conditions.

Proposition A: Approve 2025 congressional map

A yes vote would approve House Bill 1 from the 2025 extraordinary session, which redraws congressional district boundaries, and a no vote would leave the 2022 boundaries in place.
