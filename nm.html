State · Vote for one

Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials. The governor of New Mexico holds the supreme executive power of the state and sees that the laws are faithfully executed.

Greggory Hull

Republican

Mayor of Rio Rancho, running for governor.

Greggory Hull's campaign calls him the mayor of Rio Rancho. It says the city police budget more than doubled under him and that he secured more than $6 billion in investment there.

  • His Four Steps to Zero plan would phase out state tax on eligible personal income, starting with the first $25,000 for single filers and $50,000 for married couples filing jointly, while still taxing net business profits.
  • He says he would strengthen rural health care, recruit doctors and nurses, and change medical-malpractice law.
  • He says he would add police, treat fentanyl as a border problem, and use a zero-tolerance approach to violent crime and crimes against children.

Gregg Hull for Governor · Donate

Deb Haaland

Democratic

Candidate for governor and a former U.S. secretary of the interior.

Deb Haaland's campaign says she is a New Mexican who has faced homelessness and financial insecurity, built a salsa business, marked more than three decades of sobriety, and became the first Native American cabinet secretary. It says she also served in Congress.

  • She says that as governor she would concentrate on crime, schools, and prices from health care to groceries.
  • She says she would protect communities from Donald Trump and immigration enforcement so the state can focus on those problems.

Deb Haaland for New Mexico · Donate

Federal · Vote for one

Senate

The winner is one of 100 votes on federal law, spending, treaties, and judges. The term is six years. The Constitution provides two senators from each state.

Larry Marker

Republican

Republican candidate for U.S. Senate.

Larry Marker's campaign says that for nearly a decade, as a private citizen, he challenged political elites in New Mexico at personal cost. It says he is running to defend energy jobs, schools, and border security.

  • He says he would defend oil and gas, including hydrogen and nuclear development, and oppose federal permitting delays that he says threaten school funding.
  • He says the border is still a path for fentanyl and that he would back Border Patrol and local police.
  • He says farmers and ranchers should not lose grazing, water, or working land to agency enforcement.

Larry Marker for U.S. Senate · Donate

Ben Ray Lujan

Democratic

U.S. senator running for another term.

Ben Ray Luján's campaign says he filed for another Senate term and has focused on costs, health care, and internet access for New Mexico.

  • He says he has worked to lower prescription prices and to protect Medicare, Medicaid, and Social Security.
  • He says he has defended nutrition assistance for working families, seniors, veterans, and rural communities.
  • He says he has worked to connect families to the internet and to deliver results in all 33 counties.

Ben Ray Luján for U.S. Senate · Donate

State · Vote for one

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law. New Mexico gives this office primary authority over consumer-protection and antitrust enforcement, criminal appeals, some complex white-collar crimes, peace-officer training, and most natural-resource and environmental matters.

Samuel Kane Sr.

Republican

Attorney running for attorney general.

Samuel Kane Sr.'s campaign says he is an attorney with decades of experience who was not born in New Mexico and later made it home. It says he is a cyclist, a pilot, and married to Jessica, his business partner.

  • He says he would challenge license-plate reader surveillance that tracks people without a warrant.
  • He says methamphetamine and fentanyl are driving a crime crisis and that he would back officers on those cases.
  • He says he would scrutinize data-center projects and utility rate increases, and that environmental laws should be enforced so the land stays intact.

Sam Kane for Attorney General · Donate

Raul Torrez

Democratic

New Mexico's attorney general, running for another term.

Raúl Torrez's campaign says he is the state's 32nd attorney general, a former federal prosecutor, and a former district attorney for the Albuquerque area.

  • He says protecting children is his priority, including lawsuits against Meta and Snap over online abuse and investigations of the child-welfare system.
  • He says the office has pursued online predators and school harms such as cyberbullying.
  • He says he is also working on consumer rules for online markets, oversight of higher education, and protection of natural resources.

Raúl Torrez for Attorney General · Donate

State · Vote for one

Secretary of State

The secretary of state runs the duties this state assigns that office. Those duties are not the same in every state. In New Mexico, the secretary of state is the chief election officer and maintains the statewide list of registered voters.

Ramona Goolsby

Republican

Candidate for secretary of state.

Ramona Goolsby says she came to New Mexico in 2019, worked as a primary-care provider for the Veterans Administration, and is a retired family nurse practitioner. She says she serves on the Ciudad Soil and Water Conservation District board.

  • She says every eligible voter should be able to register and vote under clear rules.
  • She says public money should be managed in the open, and that trust depends on professional oversight.
  • She says her campaign is built on access, responsibility, and trust in elections.

Ramona for New Mexico · Donate

Amanda López Askin

Democratic

Doña Ana County clerk running for secretary of state.

Amanda López Askin says she is in her second term as clerk of Doña Ana County, grew up in Las Cruces, and previously worked as a family and mental health therapist.

  • She says she is running to protect the right to vote and to keep elections safe, secure, and fair.
  • She says the office should make campaign finance more transparent.
  • She says she would give small businesses clearer, more stable dealings with the state.

Amanda for New Mexico · Donate
