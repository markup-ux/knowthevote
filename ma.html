State · Vote for one

Governor

The governor signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and appoints executive officials. The governor leads the executive branch, nominates judicial officers, and serves as commander-in-chief of the state's military forces.

Michael Minogue

Republican

Business leader and Army veteran running for governor.

Mike Minogue's site describes him as a husband, father, combat veteran, and the business leader who took Abiomed from an idea to a global standard in cardiac care.

  • He says a voter-backed audit of the Legislature is not being enforced and that the political establishment is wasting tax dollars and growing debt.
  • He says taxes and regulation are pushing young people out and making it hard for seniors to stay, and he asks supporters to join a call to suspend the gas tax.
  • He says the state needs entrepreneurs and manufacturing to stay and expand, and that the governor's basic job is public safety, including enforcement against fentanyl dealers.

minogueforma.com · Donate

Maura Healey

Democratic

Governor of Massachusetts, running for re-election with Lieutenant Governor Kim Driscoll.

Maura Healey's campaign says she and Kim Driscoll were elected in 2022 as the first all-women executive team in the nation's history and are running again to keep lowering costs.

  • Her campaign says the first term ended renter's fees, capped health-care deductibles and co-pays, opposed utility rate increases, cut taxes, and protected SNAP from federal cuts.
  • It also points to work on the MBTA, free school meals, free community college, and veterans' homes.
  • For a second term she says she will defend health care and abortion rights, access to vaccines, research, jobs, and lower costs, and stand up to Donald Trump.

maurahealey.com · Donate

Andrea James

Independent

Independent candidate for governor.

Andrea James's site says her family has lived in Massachusetts for seven generations and that she is running to put the government's agenda on the needs of people.

  • She says a governor's priorities should be housing, health care, child care, education, and quality of life, and she calls housing a human right.
  • She says people should be able to see a doctor without going into debt and that families should have child care they trust.
  • She says Massachusetts should not have contracts with ICE, and she wants data centers banned in favor of lower utility costs and green energy.

ajforma.com · Donate

Federal · Vote for one

Senate

The winner is one of 100 votes on federal law, spending, treaties, and judges. The term is six years. The Senate has the sole power to try impeachments.

John Deaton

Republican

Trial lawyer and Marine veteran running for the U.S. Senate.

John Deaton's site describes him as a trial attorney, Marine veteran, cancer survivor, and father of three daughters.

  • He says affordable health care is a right and that he will work to break up large insurers and pharmacy benefit managers, lower drug prices, and expand telehealth.
  • He wants faster permitting for housing and an energy mix that includes nuclear power, and he says he will not support what he calls blank checks for foreign aid.
  • He says he will secure the border, expand legal immigration, and support an earned path to citizenship for Dreamers who meet strict standards.

johndeatonforsenate.com · Donate

Ed Markey

Democratic

U.S. senator from Massachusetts, first elected to the Senate in 2013.

Ed Markey's campaign says that since he entered the Senate he has refused to compromise progressive values and has focused on costs, opportunity, rights, and a safer and more sustainable future.

  • He says he is fighting to lower costs, protect workers, and expand opportunity in education and jobs.
  • He says health care is too expensive and that he wants lower costs, wider access, stronger public health, and corporations held accountable.
  • He also lists clean energy and transit, civil rights for LGBTQ people and immigrants, stronger gun laws, and a fairer justice system.

Other

Youth worker and community organizer running for the U.S. Senate.

Joe Tache's site says he is a socialist based in Boston who has spent more than ten years in movements against worker exploitation, war, police brutality, and gentrification, and that he writes and teaches that capitalism is the root of those problems.

Attorney General

The attorney general is the state's chief legal officer. Which cases the office files, and whether it prosecutes crimes, depends on that state's law. In Massachusetts the office protects consumers, investigates and prosecutes crime, and works on fraud, the environment, workers, and civil rights.

Michael Walsh

Republican

Massachusetts litigator running for attorney general.

Michael Walsh's site says he was born and raised in Lynnfield, has practiced law in Massachusetts for 15 years, and earned his bachelor's degree and law degree at Suffolk University at unusually young ages. It says he has represented workers, homeowners, service members, and towns, and is currently litigating against the attorney general's office.

  • He says he will enforce laws already on the books, including the legislative audit, with no selective enforcement.
  • He opposes state zoning mandates that he says override local home rule and threaten funding.
  • He says he will fight utility rate hikes, vet ballot questions for clarity and legality, and investigate fraud, waste, and abuse.

mikewalshforag.com · Donate

Andrea Campbell

Democratic

Attorney general of Massachusetts, running for re-election.

Andrea Campbell's site says she was born and raised in Roxbury and the South End, went from foster care to Princeton and into public service, and is the first Black woman to serve as the state's attorney general.

  • She says she is trying to make life more affordable by putting money back into people's pockets.
  • She says she is defending freedoms against rollbacks from the Trump era.
  • She says she is fighting for the next generation and for wider opportunity for every child, including reproductive rights, civil rights, and cases against powerful corporations.

Secretary of State

The secretary of state runs the duties this state assigns that office. Those duties are not the same in every state. Massachusetts calls this officer the secretary of the commonwealth, who keeps public records, administers elections, regulates securities, and registers corporations.

Anne Brensley

Republican

Former Wayland selectwoman and business executive running for secretary of state.

Anne Brensley's site says she is a former Wayland selectwoman, chief executive of DevCo North America, and was born in Honduras.

  • She says she wants to encourage business growth.
  • She says she will engage directly with disenfranchised voters.
  • She says she will protect the integrity of the ballot and campaign on transparency and local control.

annebrensley.com · Donate

William Galvin

Democratic

Secretary of the commonwealth, seeking another term.

William Galvin's campaign letter says he wants to continue as secretary of state through 2030 and calls himself the longest-serving state elections administrator in the country.

  • He says he is in federal court resisting efforts to limit registration and voting by mail and to obtain detailed personal information about Massachusetts voters.
  • He says he intends to expose and resist federal interference with the 2028 presidential election and the Electoral College.
  • He says he wants a complete Census count in 2030, including group quarters and residents who were not born in the United States, and he lists election integrity, early voting, vote-by-mail, consumer protection, and historical preservation.

billgalvin.com

District 1

Richard Neal

Democratic

U.S. representative and ranking member of the House Ways and Means Committee.

He represents 83 cities and towns in western and central Massachusetts and says he has spent his career serving people he has met and drawing on his own story. He says he is known as a champion of the middle class.

  • He says grocery and prescription costs are too high, and he is fighting for middle-class tax cuts, community investment, and an end to tariffs that raise prices.
  • He says he opposes wars he calls reckless because they threaten security and the economy.
  • He says he has fought to protect Social Security and Medicare and to make sure the region gets its fair share.

Nadia Milleron

Independent

Independent farmer, attorney, and advocate running for Congress.

She says she is a mother whose daughter died in the 2019 Boeing 737 MAX crash in Ethiopia, and that she then helped win passage of the Aircraft Certification, Safety, and Accountability Act. She ran in this district in 2024 and says she answers to voters rather than a party.

  • She says she would eliminate federal income tax on earnings under $100,000, stop price gouging, and vote against sending tax dollars to foreign wars.
  • She says she would bar corporations from buying elections, cap rents, and ban corporate speculators from buying local homes.
  • She says she supports health care for all that removes corporate insurers, more local doctors, and a personal term limit of three House terms.
